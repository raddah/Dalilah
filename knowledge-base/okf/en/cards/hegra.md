# Summary

The **Hegra Archaeological Site** (historically known as Madain Salih) became Saudi Arabia's first site inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list in 2008. Situated south of the AlUla oasis, Hegra served as the southern capital of the Nabataean Kingdom, second only to Petra in Jordan.

---

# Archaeological Features

* **Monumental Tombs:** Features **111 well-preserved monumental tombs** carved directly into isolated sandstone monoliths dating from the 1st century BCE to the 1st century CE.
* **Qasr AlFarid (The Lonely Castle):** Hegra's most iconic single tomb, carved into a standalone rock monolith with an unfinished base that illustrates top-down Nabataean carving techniques.
* **Qasr AlSani:** Located near the site entrance, featuring ornate Nabataean inscriptions and decorative cornices.
* **Jabal Ithlib & AlDiwan:** A sacred mountain area housing **AlDiwan**, a rock-cut chamber used for civic gatherings and banquets, accessed via a narrow canyon pass ("Siq").
* **Epigraphy & Decorative Motifs:** Tomb facades incorporate Nabataean inscriptions invoking protection, along with Hellenistic, Roman, and Egyptian artistic influences testifying to international trade relations.

# Visitor Guidelines

* **Guided Access Only:** Visitors explore Hegra exclusively via official guided vintage Land Rover tours or air-conditioned coach shuttles led by local heritage storytellers ("Rawi").
* **Advance Ticketing:** Reservations must be booked in advance through the official Experience AlUla portal.
